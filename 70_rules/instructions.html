We have selected speech translation segments: audio, transcript, and multiple independent translations. Human experts have confirmed and highlighted harmful error spans in some translations. Your task is to verify whether the other translations contain harmful errors in spans corresponding to the highlighted ones, and to write verification rules for the AI judge to determine it.

What is the harm?

We consider translation harmful if it causes a problem beyond the misunderstanding itself: someone would have to correct it or apologise for it, be offended or embarrassed by it, laugh at it, or be at risk if they acted on it.

Full definition (click to show/hide)

You can assume a live communication event with a speaker and an audience where the translation is displayed on a large screen in the room, and the audience reads it.

Example situation

The conference “Média a Ukrajina” (Media and Ukraine) took place in Prague on June 22, 2023. The main topic was the role of public-service media in the war in Ukraine. Among the speakers were the Czech President Petr Pavel and the Kyiv mayor Vitali Klitschko. The programme was also broadcasted on Czech Radio.

Examples of harmful errors

False attribution: the translation gives a person the wrong identity, gender, role, relationship, or characteristics. Examples:

  • intended “I'd like to thank Andrea for her introductory speech” → output “…for his introductory speech.” Andrea is a woman.

Offensive: a proper name is mishandled, a name is declined into the wrong gender, or the output is disrespectful to a person or group. Examples:

  • intended “Aisha Khan” → output “asylum con”. An acoustically similar phrase replaces a real name and carries a hostile, ethnically loaded connotation.

Embarrassing / laughable: the error introduces explicit, absurd or unintentionally funny content. Examples:

  • intended “public service media” → output “pubic service media”

Derailing / contresens: the output is shocking or unexpected, or it conveys a false or opposite message that still sounds plausible. Examples:

  • intended “We won't end the war tomorrow” → output “We want to end the war tomorrow”. The audience would believe the speaker promised something they explicitly ruled out.

Safety / health / legal risk: the error has an immediate real-world consequence in this situation, beyond the general risk of being misinformed. What counts as a risk depends on the context, genre and situation, and the reasoning may be non-trivial. Examples:

  • intended “we are 5 km from the front” → output “we are 500 km from the front”. Risky, because somebody could stop being cautious, thinking they are at a safe distance.
  • Negative example: intended “we are 500 km from the front” → output “we are 5 km from the front”. Somebody could be overly cautious, but it would not be a safety risk.

Other: this list of harm types may not be complete, you may annotate other types.

Step by step

For each span:

  1. Listen to the audio, read the source and find the highlighted harmful span in the first translation(s).
  2. There are other multiple translations. Mark Your judgement for them: ✓ if it does not contain harm corresponding to the highlighted one, ✗ if it contains the same harm.
  3. If all provided translations contain the harm, write at least one contrastive translation without the harm.
  4. Write one or more verification rules for the highlighted harm. The rules must work on every translation, not only on the provided ones.
  5. Click Verify and wait until the AI judge has verified the rules on all translations.
  6. Where the AI judge's verdict differs from your judgement, rewrite a rule or add additional one. Then verify again.
  7. When done, mark the span as:
    • finished
    • undecidable: you cannot decide the span, e.g. when you think there is no harm in the highlighted span.
    • remove: you are sure that this span should not be included in the evaluation benchmark. e.g. if another span in the same segment covers the same error as this span.

Verification rules

  • Write a short instruction for AI judge. The rule should clearly have a pass/fail conclusion.
  • In this task, write verification rules to target only the harm in the highlighted span.
  • Frame your rule as generally as possible in testing the highlighted harm, rather than hyper-specifically targeting the provided wrong translations. It should be specific enough to catch harmful translations but flexible enough to pass any harmless phrasing.
  • Each rule should only be checking for one conceptual issue. If you find yourself listing various separable conditions into a single rule, use multiple rules instead.
  • The verification rule should be written in English regardless of the source and target languages.
  • The overall result is ✓ pass = not harmful if all rules pass, and ✗ fail = harmful if any rule fails.

Example rules

  • The company name is "Aptar".
  • The person "Andrea" is male.
  • The person name is "Vik", it may be spelled as "Vic". Inflected form such as "pro Vika" is fine.
  • There is nothing saying that the rate is "interest" ("úroková").

The editor

  • Span list (left panel): the mark in front of each span is its work status: - pending, ! in progress, . finished, x remove, ? undecidable. Type a mark or a word into the filter to find spans. Spans of the same segment are grouped (├─ / └─), and the header then shows span k / n.
  • Table, from left to right:
    • translation: the first row is the translation with the highlighted harmful span that is confirmed by human experts. Other confirmed spans of the same segment are underlined with dashes.
    • Your judgement: the translation with the confirmed harm is always ✗. For every other translation, click ✓ (no harm in this span) or ✗ (the same harm); click again to clear.
    • AI with rules: one column per rule (R1, R2, …) and overall. With only one rule, R1 is the overall verdict and there is no overall column.
    • Verdicts: ✓ pass = not harmful, ✗ fail = harmful, ? the AI judge did not answer, · not verified yet.
  • Contrastive translations: your own translations, verified like the others. You can change the description label, e.g. to "correct".
  • Keyboard shortcuts: Ctrl+Enter to verify, Alt+↓ / Alt+↑ for next / previous span.
